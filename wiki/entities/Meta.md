# Meta

Hyperscaler whose data-centre financing structures have become the template for the AI build-out — and are now showing their first fragilities.

## Recent developments

- **2026-08-15** — The forced **Manus unwind** nears completion: investors buy the AI-agent company back at the same ~$2bn; Tencent becomes largest shareholder; Beijing to lift the founders' travel ban ([[2026-08-15 China poised to lift travel ban on Manus founders]]).
- **2026-08-16** — Its Richland Parish, Louisiana data centre is being powered by **10 new gas plants** (Entergy); El Paso and Richland Parish both start on off-grid gas ([[2026-08-16 Big Tech's data centre boom poised to drive up carbon emissions]]).
- **2026-08-17** — Its $14bn **Sopaipilla** campus in El Paso (co-owned with BlackRock) is revealed to carry only fractional insurance: ~$450mn all-risk cover on a $14bn asset, no total-loss cover, and a penalty-free lease exit for Meta if a severe casualty delays the project 18+ months. Ratings (S&P A+, Fitch/KBRA AA-) rest on broker risk models; the broker (Marsh) both advises the parties and sells the cover ([[2026-08-17 Meta and BlackRock's $14bn data centre exposes lenders to insurance gap]]).

## Background

- Pioneered the off-balance-sheet lease structure: the **Beignet** JV with Blue Owl owns its "Hyperion" Louisiana site; Meta holds 20% but guarantees a 20+ year lease, letting Beignet issue a $27bn amortising bond that isn't Meta's debt on paper. Other hyperscalers copied it ([[2026-08-10 Just how big is the hidden leverage of AI hyperscalers]]). See [[AI infrastructure financing]].
