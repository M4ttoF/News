# China's economic slowdown

**Where this stands:** July data confirmed the slowdown is deepening — industrial output and retail sales both missed, fixed asset investment is down 6.7% for the year, and Q2 GDP (4.3%) was the weakest non-Covid reading since GDP reporting began in the early 1990s, below the official 4.5–5% target band. Beijing has promised accelerated fiscal spending; what growth exists is increasingly confined to state-aligned sectors, with the tech/AI stack the standout.

## Developments

- **2026-08-17** — July data: industrial output +4.5% (missing 4.8% forecast), retail sales +0.6% (trade-in subsidies now a drag; Goldman sees just 1.5% retail growth for 2026), fixed asset investment −6.7% Jan–Jul. Growth is "becoming more visibly policy-driven" (Conference Board): information transmission investment +26%, electronics manufacturing +7.8%, while property and private investment shrink. Exports (+23.9%) carry the economy ([[2026-08-17 China investment slump deepens]]).
- **2026-07 (reported)** — Politburo agreed to "accelerate the pace of fiscal expenditure"; Xi Jinping: "tap the potential of domestic demand" ([[2026-08-17 China investment slump deepens]]).

## Watching

- Whether fiscal acceleration shows up in August–September data.
- The "next China shock" thesis (Lizzi Lee, Asia Society) is now fetched and tracked in [[China's open-weight AI push]]: weak domestic demand makes exporting cheap open-weight AI a revenue necessity — the slowdown and the AI push are one story ([[2026-08-17 The next China shock will come from open-source AI]]).
- Property prices "yet to find a bottom" (Goldman).
