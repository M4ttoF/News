# Data centres — power, carbon and geography

**Where this stands:** The build-out's physical externalities are now quantified: the FT counts 101.5mn tonnes of CO2 a year from the 60 largest planned US data centres (~7% of US power-sector emissions), with three-quarters of serving utilities adding gas capacity and coal retirements slipping. Public opposition is broad (70%+ of Americans oppose local data centres). Meanwhile the geography is shifting — Malaysia's Johor is the exemplar winner of AI supply-chain diversification.

## Developments

- **2026-08-16** — The FT emissions analysis: 101.5mn t/yr; utilities' gas pivot (Entergy's 10 plants for Meta Richland Parish; Georgia Power's coal life-extensions); Amazon +16% / Microsoft +25% / Alphabet +18% reported emissions growth; behind-the-meter gas as the stopgap ([[2026-08-16 Big Tech's data centre boom poised to drive up carbon emissions]]).
- **2026-08-16** — Malaysia's Q2 GDP +6% on chips and data centres; Johor = 76 of 187 national data centres; FDI +41%; frictions: water/power protests and chip-smuggling pressure ([[2026-08-16 Malaysia profits from data centre boom]]).
