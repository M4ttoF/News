# AI in courts and law enforcement

**Where this stands:** AI is entering the legal process from both ends, and both are causing trouble. UK police forces are now disciplining and criminally investigating officers for using chatbots on case material — including a detective chief inspector accused of AI-maintained decision logs, which are disclosable to defence lawyers. Meanwhile members of the public are using AI to generate legal arguments that cite statute confidently but sometimes wrongly, shifting cost onto whoever has to answer them. The common failure mode is the same: fluent text that is not reliably true, entering a process that assumes text is considered.

## Developments

### Police use

- **2026-08-10** — A **Derbyshire detective chief inspector** faces an IOPC gross-misconduct investigation, referred by the force on 2026-06-23; the case reportedly involves using AI to maintain **decision logs** disclosable to defence. The CPS has notified defence lawyers in affected cases. This is the most senior UK police figure known to face an AI-misuse investigation ([[2026-08-10 Senior UK detective under investigation for alleged misuse of AI]]).
- **2026-08-10** — A **Gloucestershire police staff member** was dismissed — the first UK police-staff dismissal over AI misuse — for using a chatbot for police work from October 2024 and **circumventing the force's December 2024 ChatGPT block**. Notably, the confidentiality count did not hold up: investigation found all information entered had been publicly available ([[2026-08-10 Senior UK detective under investigation for alleged misuse of AI]]).
- **2026-06** — Derbyshire opened a **criminal investigation** into a more junior detective for alleged perverting the course of justice involving chatbots used to prepare court material ([[2026-08-10 Senior UK detective under investigation for alleged misuse of AI]]).

### Public and consumer use

- **2026-08-11** — UK letting agents report **90% of complaints are now multi-page, statute-quoting emails** (Martyn Gerrard), some citing provisions from **draft versions of the Renters' Rights Act that never became law**. Propertymark wants AI assistance declared to the Property Ombudsman. Shelter confirms AI platforms make "false, incomplete or misleading statements" about the Act, while defending tenants' right to understand new protections ([[2026-08-11 UK letting agents under pressure from AI-assisted tenant complaints]]).

## Tension worth flagging

The letting-agent story has an obvious asymmetry: agents object to AI-assisted complaints while themselves using AI to summarise those complaints and cut labour costs ([[2026-08-11 UK letting agents under pressure from AI-assisted tenant complaints]]). A disclosure rule that applies only to complainants would formalise that asymmetry — worth watching if the Ombudsman takes up the proposal.

## Watching

- IOPC outcome for the Derbyshire DCI, and whether any prosecutions collapse over tainted decision logs.
- Whether other UK forces disclose similar cases, or issue clearer AI guidance rather than outright blocks (the Gloucestershire case suggests blocks get circumvented).
- Whether the Property Ombudsman adopts an AI-disclosure requirement.
