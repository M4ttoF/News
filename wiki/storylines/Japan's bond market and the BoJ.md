# Japan's bond market and the BoJ

**Where this stands:** The 10-year JGB yield hit 2.93% on August 17 — a three-decade high — closing in on 3%, the rate assumed in the government budget and thus "a critical defence line for fiscal credibility." A weak yen (~¥159/$ despite recent co-ordinated US-Japan intervention) is driving inflation expectations toward 2%, and markets expect a BoJ hike to 1.25% in September even though Q2 growth badly missed.

## Developments

- **2026-08-17** — 10-year yield touches **2.93%**, highest since 1996. Deutsche Bank's Omori: crossing 3% "would signify an interest rate rise unforeseen by the government." Same day: Q2 GDP +1.1% annualised vs 2% expected, private consumption and capex both down — yet markets still price a September hike, and an accelerating pace after ([[2026-08-17 Japan's 10-year bond yield hits three-decade high]]).
- **2026-07 (end)** — Co-ordinated **US-Japan interventions** pulled the yen from a 40-year low of ¥164/$ to almost ¥155; half the gain has since unwound to ~¥159 ([[2026-08-17 Japan's 10-year bond yield hits three-decade high]]).

## Watching

- The September BoJ decision (hike to 1.25% priced in) against the weak Q2 GDP print.
- The 3% line on the 10-year and any fiscal-credibility repricing.
- Oil from the Iran war as an imported-inflation channel — the same war straining US Asian alliances ([[US-Korea alliance under Trump]]).
