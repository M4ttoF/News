# AI infrastructure financing

**Where this stands:** The AI build-out has outgrown public markets, so private capital is now the funding mechanism — and the deals are escalating fast. In one week of August 2026 the scale jumped from a $35bn Apollo/Blackstone structure to [[Nvidia]] convening six of the largest asset managers to raise **over $500bn**, with Nvidia itself backstopping up to a quarter of project residual value. Meanwhile the obligations already contracted are larger and less visible than the headline balance sheets suggest: Goldman counts **$1.5tn of hyperscaler lease commitments, $1tn of which never appears in the financial statements**, plus nearly **$1tn more in purchase commitments** per Morgan Stanley. The open question is who absorbs the loss if the depreciation schedule on AI hardware turns out to be shorter than the loan terms.

## Developments

- **2026-08-10** — The hidden-leverage tally. Hyperscalers genuinely do look conservative on conventional metrics (**0.5× net leverage**, more cash than debt), but Goldman Sachs finds **$1.5tn in aggregate lease commitments — $1.0tn from leases not yet commenced** and therefore absent from the statements under GAAP ASC 842. Five years ago the total was ~$200bn. Morgan Stanley counts a further **$982bn of purchase commitments** (compute, chips, equipment, electricity) across Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Nvidia and Oracle. Fitch and Moody's exclude uncommenced leases from their metrics; S&P includes them where "debt-like in substance" ([[2026-08-10 Just how big is the hidden leverage of AI hyperscalers]]).

- **2026-08-11** — [[Nvidia]] forms a financing partnership with **Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR** for **$500bn+** in "compute financing platforms," funding its own ecosystem "at attractive rates for Nvidia customers." Huang offers a **"residual-value support mechanism" for up to 25%** of financed project value — the credit enhancement that unlocks insurance and pension money. Rationale: hyperscaler/neocloud spending "may reach over $8tn in the coming years," beyond what public markets can absorb ([[2026-08-11 Nvidia becomes the bank of AI]]).
- **2026-08-04 (approx., reported the prior week)** — A complex **$35bn** AI financing structured by Apollo and Blackstone; the FT's *Due Diligence* predicted such deals "may grow larger yet" — confirmed within days ([[2026-08-11 Nvidia becomes the bank of AI]]).
- **2026-08-01** — The bear case, via Lex: data-centre builders may invest **~$9tn by 2030**, heavily debt-financed, creating a "temporal mismatch" (Man Group's phrase) in which chips wear out or are made obsolete before loans are repaid. Efficiency gains or cheap Chinese models could strand capacity ([[2026-08-01 Situational Awareness got the future right]]).

## The template: Meta's Beignet

The structure everyone is copying. Rather than issuing bonds itself for its **"Hyperion"** data centre in Louisiana, Meta formed a joint venture with **Blue Owl** called **Beignet** that develops and owns the site. Meta holds only **20%** of Beignet but committed to lease Hyperion for **at least 20 years** — a guarantee solid enough to let Beignet issue an amortising **$27bn bond**. The debt is not Meta's debt on paper, though Meta is on the hook for the payments. Other hyperscalers judged this a tremendous idea and lease structures loosely modelled on it are now the favoured method ([[2026-08-10 Just how big is the hidden leverage of AI hyperscalers]]).

Market check: the Beignet bond yields **6.95%**, up from a **5.65%** low at issuance last autumn — a modest sell-off, partly explained by rising Treasury yields rather than AI-specific fear ([[2026-08-10 Just how big is the hidden leverage of AI hyperscalers]]).

## The structural shift

Firms once known as "mercenary leveraged buyout shops" — Apollo, Blackstone, KKR — have repositioned as "regular-way financiers" to AI, energy and infrastructure, deploying money from insurers, pensions and retail savers seeking returns slightly above investment-grade bonds ([[2026-08-11 Nvidia becomes the bank of AI]]). That routes household savings into AI capex risk.

The same firms occupy three roles at once, which is worth holding in view: **Blackstone and Apollo have deals with [[Anthropic]]; Bain, TPG and Brookfield have formed a venture with [[OpenAI]]** — so they are lenders to the AI build-out, deployers of AI inside their portfolios, and owners of businesses AI may disrupt ([[2026-08-10 Boehly's Eldridge rolls out AI across portfolio]]).

## Watching

- Whether the Nvidia partnership reaches final agreement, and what the residual-value guarantee looks like in contract form.
- The un-fetched underlying FT scoop on the Nvidia raise (still paywalled in our fetch queue).
- Whether a vendor financing its own customers draws scrutiny — the pattern has precedents that ended badly (telecom vendor financing, 1999–2001).
- Whether uncommenced lease commitments keep compounding at the current rate — Goldman's estimate rose from $1.2tn to $1.5tn in a single month.
- Whether Fitch and Moody's move toward S&P's more conservative treatment of uncommenced leases. That's a ratings-methodology change that would reprice a lot of paper.
