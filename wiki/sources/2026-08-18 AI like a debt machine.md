# AI: like a debt machine

**Unhedged newsletter (commentary/analysis)** — the corporate bond market is being remade by hyperscaler AI borrowing; framing question: "are hyperscaler bonds the new Treasuries?"

The numbers:
- US investment-grade issuance **already past $1.5tn this year** (Goldman) — 2026 on track to beat the pandemic-era record; Goldman's $2.1tn full-year forecast now looks conservative. Average deal $1.7bn (biggest post-crisis); **20+ "jumbo" deals of $10bn+** at announcement vs 12 in all of 2025 and 15 across 2022–24. Two-thirds of jumbo value (this year and last) is **tech, not M&A**.
- Duration: big-five hyperscaler paper averages **16.5-year tenor** vs 10.7 for US IG overall — a new duration supply competing with long Treasuries, per Barclays one of the reasons **global yields keep rising despite weak US data** (alongside the ~$2tn US deficit and price-sensitive auction buyers).
- Strain signals: Pimco (Karoui) finds Amazon/Alphabet **dollar bonds underperforming their euro twins** — same issuer, same fundamentals — "demand fatigue in the dollar IG market." Index concentration now makes IG indices hyperscaler-sensitive, like equities.
- The relief valve: Apollo's Sløk expects **$1tn+ of financing to migrate to private placements/infrastructure debt/asset-backed structures** — i.e., straight into [[Private credit stress]] territory and the [[AI infrastructure financing]] machinery.

The macro link the wiki should hold onto: hyperscaler duration supply pushing up long yields connects the AI build-out to [[Japan's bond market and the BoJ]] and to the chip-stock selloff on borrowing costs ([[2026-08-18 US chip stocks slide as government borrowing costs hit multiyear highs]]). Feeds [[AI infrastructure financing]] and [[AI investment boom and bubble fears]].
