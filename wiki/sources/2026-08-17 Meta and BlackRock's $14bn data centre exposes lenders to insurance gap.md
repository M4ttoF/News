# Meta and BlackRock's $14bn data centre exposes lenders to insurance gap

The **$14bn "Sopaipilla"** data centre campus in El Paso, Texas — owned by [[Meta]] and BlackRock — is only fractionally insured, leaving the venture (and its bondholders) exposed to billions in losses above the coverage caps. Full coverage for mega-projects has become **prohibitively expensive**; the project is not insured against total loss.

The coverage, per people familiar (advised by broker Marsh):
- All-risk property: up to **$427mn during construction / $450mn in operation** (premium ~$5mn/yr, escalating 2%/yr) — against a $14bn asset.
- Terrorism: **$645mn**. Rent abatement on construction delay: **$218mn**. General liability: **$50mn** per event and aggregate (premium ~$1mn/yr).
- Marsh's "probable maximum loss" basis: a 1-in-250 to 1-in-500-year regional fire — i.e. a **3.9–7.7% chance of at least one such event during Meta's 20-year lease**.

Why it matters beyond one site: lenders to smaller data centres demand full insurance, but as deals ballooned, **partial coverage became acceptable** — brokers now advise accepting losses above modelled maxima. S&P rated Sopaipilla's debt A+ (one notch below Meta) noting "Meta has to pay the gap" up to $450mn — and that Meta can **terminate the lease penalty-free** if a severe casualty delays the project 18+ months, pulling the rug from under the debt. Fitch/KBRA rated it AA-, same as Meta corporate. Covington's Marc Norman flags broker conflicts: Marsh both advises multiple parties and sells the insurance.
