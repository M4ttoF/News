# AI's 'cookie banner' moment: EU labels come for the bots

European companies scramble ahead of **August 2, 2026**, when the [[EU AI Act]]'s first consumer-facing transparency obligations become enforceable: firms must disclose when customers interact with AI and label AI-generated images/audio/video. Fines become possible from the same date.

**Key facts**
- "This will be a cookie banner moment for AI" — Cooley lawyer Patrick Van Eecke. Guidance was published only weeks before the deadline.
- Business pushback: Zalando (AI generates/enhances ~90% of its marketing material) calls it "another unnecessary layer"; Eurocommerce warns of indiscriminate labelling; CCIA Europe says deepfakes are defined so broadly the label "stops meaning anything".
- Some see upside: Booking.com says AI-disclosure measurably improves customer-service outcomes; Chess.com notes 250mn+ games/month are played against bots and users want to know.
- Also from Aug 2: Commission gains powers to inspect general-purpose AI models posing "systemic risks" (fines up to 3% of global turnover or €15mn). High-risk system requirements already postponed to December 2027.
