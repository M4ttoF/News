# Can AI improve my parenting?

Robert Shrimsley, FT Weekend Magazine column. **Opinion, not reporting.** Uses a mocked Sam Altman social-media post to argue that AI promotion is built on "you don't have to bother with this any more" — and that parenting is the wrong place to apply it.

**Key facts**
- The trigger: Altman posted about a "cool use of chatgpt" — connect family calendars, explain your kids' interests, and **every morning on the school drive ChatGPT generates a podcast** covering the kid's soccer game that afternoon, an upcoming birthday, some news. He was widely roasted; a standard reply was "or you could just try talking to your kids."
- Altman has an 18-month-old son and said last year: "I cannot imagine having gone through figuring out how to raise a newborn without ChatGPT." Shrimsley's reply: "We coped."
- The argument: much AI promotion strips down to *you don't have to bother with this any more* — research, data analytics, coding, medical breakthroughs, "overseeing the nation's nuclear deterrent" — and now parenting.
- Historical parallel drawn: *Baby Einstein* / *Baby Mozart* DVDs, marketed to ease guilt about parking kids in front of the television by implying brain-power gains. "Reader, I'm not sure it worked."
- Core worry, stated as broader than parenting: AI's development "seems to lie with people who see their core mission as taking humans out of the picture, who believe that humanity would be better without people."
- Concedes genuinely useful uses: managing playdate diaries, monitoring social-media usage.
- Closing line invokes *Brave New World* as a parenting manual.

**Note for the wiki:** this is a columnist's opinion; the only checkable fact is the Altman post and the reaction to it. Useful as commentary colour, not as evidence of anything about AI capability.
