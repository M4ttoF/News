# Palantir forecasts greater demand from US groups for its AI software

[[Palantir]] raised full-year guidance (revenue ≥ $8.15bn, US commercial $3.4bn) on strong Q2: US sales +23% to $1.57bn, net income $1.06bn. Shares +12% after hours, having fallen 25% YTD on fears of disruption by AI labs.

**Key facts**
- CEO Alex Karp pitches Palantir as the "AI sovereignty" play: customers want control of their data rather than ceding it to AI labs whose products may later compete with them. Swipes at a "tiny group of people" trying to "colonise" customers on moral grounds — read as aimed at [[Anthropic]] / Dario Amodei (who warns of white-collar displacement and has a protracted legal dispute with the Pentagon over defence-use limits).
- The sovereignty pitch cuts both ways internationally: France's DGSI signed ChapsVision (with Mistral-powered assistant), Germany's armed forces excluded Palantir, UK contracts under scrutiny, Denmark/Netherlands seeking to uncouple. International revenue share down to ~19% (from 26% in 2025).
- Commercial deals (e.g. Kirkland & Ellis AI legal system) are dwarfed by the $10bn decade-long Pentagon army agreement.
