# Business development companies are paying more to borrow. But why?

FT Alphaville (analysis/commentary) on a Federal Reserve note by Sharjil Haque and Jessie Wang, studying bank lending to BDCs via confidential FR Y-14Q loan-level data — from a **monetary-policy-transmission** angle, not the usual financial-stability one.

The Fed finding:
- Outside tightening cycles, BDCs and comparable non-BDC borrowers pay similar bank loan rates (controlling for banks' internal credit assessments). **During hiking cycles, BDC loan rates balloon.**
- Not credit risk: banks' own assessments didn't deteriorate, and they reported *lower* loss-given-default on BDC loans (senior claims, collateral). The premium "is unlikely to reflect only compensation for credit risk."
- Their explanation: **bank bargaining power**. Top 3 banks hold ~half the BDC funding market, top 10 >80%; BDCs almost never switch banks; credit expansion happens by renegotiating existing lines, where incumbents set the price. Monopsony rents, extracted precisely when BDCs most need funding.

Alphaville's complication: bond markets charge BDCs a lot too — spreads above SpaceX, Oracle, and average double-B — and turned on BDCs at about the same time bank pricing jumped. So causation is murky: bank squeeze spilling into bonds, or both markets pricing **default correlation** in concentrated middle-market books (heavy software exposure, "ripe for AI disruption") that individual loan spreads don't capture.

The kicker: the entire BDC pitch is superior expertise in negotiating debt — and they may be overpaying for their own funding by never shopping around.

Context: ~half the median BDC's assets are debt-financed (43% asset-weighted); BDCs ≈ a quarter of middle-market private credit.
