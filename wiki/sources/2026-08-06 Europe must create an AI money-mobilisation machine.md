# Europe must create an AI money-mobilisation machine

Opinion/analysis. Argues Europe's AI problem is not talent or even regulation but **capital mobilisation** — and that the private sector, not just governments, is failing.

**The pattern being warned about**
- Europe invents, others exploit: the World Wide Web, MP3 audio compression, lithium-ion batteries, high-efficiency solar cells. The question is whether AI repeats it — and for now, that seems "extraordinarily unlikely" to break.
- European researchers helped pioneer modern AI (**Jürgen Schmidhuber, Geoffrey Hinton, Demis Hassabis**) while **US and Chinese companies** developed and deployed it.
- Current split: US proprietary frontier models (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) versus Chinese smaller/adaptable open models (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi). Combined with control over chip design and data centres, the two superpowers dominate the global AI stack.

**The stakes, per the "Europe 2031" essay**
- Essay title: *"Europe 2031: What Getting AI Wrong Means for Us"*, by AI researchers and investors. Warning: unless Europe changes trajectory it will **"lose the ability to shape its own future"** — politically and economically sidelined, unable to defend its values or sustain its welfare states.
- The authors judge the current European response **10 to 100 times too small**, and call for the "most ambitious political agenda in the history of postwar Europe."

**What's actually going right**
- Talented researchers and entrepreneurs; a dynamic early-stage start-up ecosystem.
- Both the EU and UK are focused on **sovereign capabilities**, particularly AI-enabled defence tech, with publicly backed scale-up funds.
- A strong industrial base gives Europe a real shot in **robotics and physical AI**.
- **Peter Sarlin** (Finnish entrepreneur; built and sold **Silo AI for $665mn**) argues Europe should flourish by **applying** AI across every sector — that's where you own the customer relationship and the data flywheel. His family office **PostScriptum** backs Legora, Lovable, CuspAI, NestAI and Advanced Machine Intelligence. His latest start-up **QuTwo** develops quantum processing units (QPUs) — the bet being that QPUs could accelerate the next computing transition as GPUs did generative AI.
- Contrarian angle on regulation: the biggest constraint on deploying AI is **public distrust**, which could become a European advantage if it strikes the right balance. **Meredith Whittaker** (president of the Signal Foundation) — who raises privacy, security, sovereignty, autonomy and agency concerns about Big Tech models — has joined PostScriptum as an adviser on trustworthy AI, on the argument that a **democratically accountable AI governance regime** could let Europe leapfrog.

**The capital indictment**
- Only **60 of the 383 recommendations** in Mario Draghi's 2024 European competitiveness report have been fully implemented, per the European Policy Innovation Council's July report.
- The failure is not only public: corporate leaders, capital-market bosses and fund managers must tackle the **€800bn annual investment shortfall** Draghi identified.
- Core claim: the genius of American and Chinese capitalism is directing torrents of capital at fast-evolving technologies. That produces wasteful over-investment and bubbles — but Europe still needs its own money-mobilisation machine.

**Note:** this is the mirror image of [[AI infrastructure financing]]. The US story is that private capital is mobilising *too* aggressively for AI; the European story is that it cannot mobilise at all. Both are in the wiki as of the same week.
