# Humanoid robots don't deserve their superhuman valuations (Lex)

**Lex column (commentary).** Unitree — Hangzhou maker of humanoid and quadruped robots, backers incl. Tencent and DeepSeek — gained **460% on its Shanghai debut**: $900mn raised at a $9bn IPO valuation (27x this year's sales), now a **$51bn market cap trading at 150x+ sales** vs Tesla's 13x. Retail demand was thousands of times the float.

Lex's deflation: Unitree sold 28,000 robots last year, mostly cheap robot dogs; its 5,215 humanoids were largely R&D platforms. Humanoids are too expensive for industrial use, manage a couple of hours per charge, and "are not about to stack the dishwasher." A ninefold output increase would be needed just to match Tesla's EV/sales. Part of the froth is structural: Beijing's tight IPO gate ($33bn raised YTD) concentrates retail money on the few listings allowed. The TAM chorus: Morgan Stanley ~1bn units by 2050; UBS 300mn/$1.7tn; Musk calling Optimus "probably the biggest product ever" while cutting car production to build it.
