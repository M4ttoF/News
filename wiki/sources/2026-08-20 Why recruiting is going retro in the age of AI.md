# Why recruiting is going retro in the age of AI

**Newsletter (Working It)** — only the lead segment ingested; the rest is a link roundup (its top story is [[2026-08-19 Is AI really responsible for recent job cuts]]).

The lead: AI has broken recruitment's signal economy. **87% of UK recruiters see AI use in at least half of applications** (Greenhouse); half call low-quality volume their biggest problem; <7% of applicants reach interview. "When every application is polished, polish tells you nothing." Wharton's Kessler: cover letters are now "cheap talk" — zero information content.

The retro fix: **recommendations and referrals** — costly signals AI can't fake, because the recommender stakes reputation ("the signalling value comes from the fact that the person was willing to send it" — even if AI drafted it). Wharton's NYC youth-employment RCT: randomly assigned recommendation letters → faster job-finding, better industry match. The catch (Greenhouse): network hiring "reinforces existing inequities, and referrals tend to give you more of the same."

Feeds [[AI and white-collar work]] — a clean case of the episode-2 thesis: AI collapsed the cost of producing application text, so the market repriced toward signals that stay expensive. Same mechanism as the tenant-complaint and bug-bounty stories.
