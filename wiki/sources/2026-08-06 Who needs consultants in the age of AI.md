# Who needs consultants in the age of AI?

Robert Armstrong column on whether strategic consulting (McKinsey, Bain, BCG) survives AI. The analytic/research work consultants once charged for — industry research, pattern-spotting, slide-deck strategy — is exactly what LLMs commoditize. The consultants' defense: the real product is *process*, not intelligence — building consensus, extracting honest information from misaligned executives, orchestrating organizational change ("this is a contact sport").

**Key points**
- Economist Luis Garicano frames it as an information problem: the knowledge needed for corporate transformation is tacit, dispersed, and often hidden — the training data for an AI planner doesn't exist until humans struggle through planning. Compares AI-planning optimists to Hayek's central planners.
- Even on the bullish-for-consultants view, the industry shrinks: the analytic "bulk" of the meal is gone, leaving only the "special sauce"; fee models are shifting from per-diem to outcome-based.
- Implementation-focused consultancies (Accenture, TCS, Capgemini) show AI-disruption worry in their share prices too.
