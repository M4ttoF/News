# AI phobia is America's new consensus

**Opinion column** — commentary, not evidence, but with load-bearing data points. Thesis: America, historically the most pro-technology society on earth, has turned Luddite — and its politics hasn't caught up.

The numbers cited: **~3/4 of Americans don't trust businesses to use AI responsibly** (Gallup); **70%+ oppose data centres in their area** (water/electricity inflation fears); **80%+ distrust AI** for driving, medical advice, hiring. Fear of AI is "the most bipartisan issue since beer" (NYT). Bannon calls data centres "weapons labs"; MTG calls Big Tech "Skynet."

The market-rivalry decoder (the column's sharpest tool): safety talk maps onto competitive position. Winners want regulation and closed weights — **"notably Anthropic, which is preparing for AI's largest-ever initial public offering"** wants "an FDA for AI"; laggards (Meta/Zuckerberg) find religion in open weights and warn about concentration. "Telling people that your company's technology is particularly dangerous is another way of saying that your stock will outperform."

Politics: Trump is "uncharacteristically deaf" to voter fear — Musk's ~$100mn midterm pledge and industry money cited; any US regulation dismissed as "helping China." Middle powers (Singapore, UAE, Europe) resist with-us-or-against-us AI-bloc ultimatums — they want both stacks, which is exactly the demand side of [[China's open-weight AI push]].

Key fact to track: **Anthropic preparing AI's largest-ever IPO** — pairs with OpenAI's own IPO scramble ([[2026-08-15 OpenAI upheaval mounts as Sam Altman readies IPO push]]); the two labs are now racing to list.
