# Seoul rattled by Trump's threat to drills at heart of US alliance

Analysis companion to [[2026-08-17 Trump orders Pentagon to scale back South Korea drills]]: the order landed **hours before the drills began** — thousands of personnel were already staged from Camp Humphreys to bases near the border. The exercises started on schedule Monday; neither government specified what gets cut.

What the drills are: Ulchi Freedom Shield, held since **1976**; this year 14 exercises over 11 days — drone attacks, GPS jamming, electronic and cyber warfare. Analysts call them the cornerstone of readiness against a nuclear-armed North.

The pattern of alliance strain this joins (worth keeping as a list — it recurs):
- March: Pentagon **relocated parts of the Thaad missile-defence system** out of Korea toward the Middle East.
- Seoul complained it wasn't briefed on a US F-16 exercise from Osan flying near China's air-defence zone.
- USS George Washington strike group leaving → "the western Pacific without an American aircraft carrier for the foreseeable future" (John Lee, Korea Risk Group).
- Trump officials frustrated over the $350bn investment pledge follow-through.

Reads on motive: an olive branch to Kim (Victor Cha, CSIS) — Trump posted a photo with Kim Saturday ("get along GREAT") and claims Kim responded to outreach. But Kim "has no compelling need to meet now given all the support from Russia and China," and may wait past US midterms for leverage. North Korea has constitutionally **dropped reunification** and treats the South as a hostile state; Seoul risks being frozen out of any US-DPRK talks, with its own security used as a bargaining chip (Choi Eun-joo, Sejong Institute).

Consequence to watch: pressure on President Lee Jae Myung to accelerate **wartime operational control (Opcon) transfer** — pledged before his term ends in 2030, possibly as early as next year.
