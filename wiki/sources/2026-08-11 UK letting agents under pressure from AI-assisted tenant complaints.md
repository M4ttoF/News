# UK letting agents under pressure from AI-assisted tenant complaints

James Pickford. UK letting agents report a flood of long, legally-argued, AI-assisted tenant complaints — often citing statute speciously — and want AI use disclosed to the Property Ombudsman.

**Key facts**
- **Propertymark** (letting-agent industry body) says assessing and resolving the complaints strains agents and landlords, who need legal knowledge and capacity to answer them properly.
- **Greg Tsuman** (managing director of lettings, Martyn Gerrard; former Arla Propertymark president): **90% of complaints are now multi-page emails** with multiple components and quoted statute — a shift over the past two to three years. Complaints that "might previously have been resolved by a short telephone call."
- Tsuman on the mechanism: AI's "predisposition to *tell you what you want to hear*" can falsely convince people they have grounds where none exist. "We have seen a spike in complex and intricate complaints... complaints that are sometimes without grounds."
- **Ben Stokes** (lettings director, JNP): AI-generated complaints sometimes cite **provisions from earlier draft versions of the Renters' Rights Act that never became law**. "AI tools are empowering people to complain about things and blow small situations out of proportion."
- Proposed remedy: Tsuman wants Property Ombudsman rules changed so complainants must **declare whether AI assisted** the complaint.
- **Shelter** (housing charity) pushes back on framing: renters fought years for the Act's protections and it's "vital that tenants understand how to exercise these new rights" (CEO Sarah Elliott) — while confirming its advice teams have seen generative AI make "false, incomplete or misleading statements" about the Act. Recommends fact-checked sources (Shelter, Gov.uk).
- Both-sides note: agents are themselves using AI to cut labour costs on admin — Martyn Gerrard uses it to **summarise the long complaints** it receives.
