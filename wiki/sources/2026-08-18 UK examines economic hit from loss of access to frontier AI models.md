# UK examines economic hit from loss of access to frontier AI models

The Cabinet Office has ordered an urgent assessment (weeks, not months) of what happens to the UK economy and security if Britons lose access to frontier models from Anthropic and OpenAI.

The trigger: **Trump's June directive demanding foreign access to Anthropic's Fable 5 be rescinded**. Anthropic's response was to pull the model **globally** — it "couldn't meaningfully distinguish between nationals and foreigners" — before relaunching it. That episode alarmed the National Security Secretariat: a cutting-edge model could be unavailable in Britain while available elsewhere.

The stakes as officials frame them:
- Economic: "For the UK to be left behind by not having access — even for a couple of weeks — would cause a hit to the economy."
- Security: cyber vulnerabilities in British companies could be exploited by *foreign users of frontier models* before being patched — the offensive-capability asymmetry from [[AI agents break containment]] applied at national level.
- Ciaran Martin (ex-NCSC) is half-reassuring — "slightly implausible that an American AI model would be restricted from foreigners" — and half not: "The US's ability to manipulate and leverage the whole AI stack — to try and force countries to bend to its will — could be significant."
- Onwurah (Commons science committee): assessment is good, but where's the plan for "unacceptable levels of dependency"? UK counterweights: £1.1bn AI hardware plan, £500mn sovereign AI fund, a cabinet-level AI minister.
