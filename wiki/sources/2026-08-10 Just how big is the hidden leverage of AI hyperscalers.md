# Just how big is the hidden leverage of AI hyperscalers?

FT Alphaville. The bull case for hyperscaler debt — strong balance sheets, more cash than debt — is accurate as far as it goes, but **roughly $2tn of AI-related obligations sit outside the conventional debt figures**, about half of it invisible in the financial statements entirely.

**The bull case, stated fairly**
- Hyperscalers have an average **net leverage ratio of 0.5×**, versus 0.8× for the technology sector and 1.8× for non-financial US companies generally (Morgan Stanley). They hold **more cash than debt**.
- So if the data centres disappoint, the argument goes, that's an equity-investor problem, not a lender problem.

**The off-balance-sheet counterweight**
- **Goldman Sachs tracked $1.5tn in aggregate lease commitments** across the hyperscaler sector using current earnings-season data. **$1.0tn of that relates to leases that have not yet commenced** — and under US GAAP those don't appear in the financial statements at all, despite being legally binding future payments.
- Trajectory: total lease commitments were **~$200bn five years ago**. Goldman's own estimate one month earlier was $1.2tn with $750bn uncommenced — so the figure is moving fast even between estimates.
- **Morgan Stanley separately counted $982bn of purchase commitments** — computing power, chips, equipment, electricity — across Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Nvidia and Oracle at the end of Q1. Also not conventional debt.
- Goldman's assessment: this treatment "can understate leverage and future liquidity needs."

**Why the accounting works this way**
- **GAAP ASC 842**: lease obligations hit the balance sheet only when the lease *starts* (at which point an offsetting right-of-use asset also appears). Binding but not-yet-commenced leases go in the footnotes.
- Rating agencies split: **Fitch and Moody's** don't count leases in their metrics until commenced; **S&P** is more conservative and includes them where it judges them "debt-like in substance."

**The structure that started it**
- Meta's **"Hyperion"** data centre in Louisiana: rather than issuing bonds itself, Meta formed a JV with **Blue Owl** called **Beignet**, which develops and owns Hyperion. Meta holds only **20%** of Beignet but committed to lease Hyperion for **at least 20 years**. That guarantee let Beignet issue an amortising **$27bn bond** — debt Meta is effectively on the hook for that does not appear as Meta's debt.
- Other hyperscalers copied the approach; lease structures loosely modelled on Beignet are now the favoured method.

**Market signal**
- The Beignet bond yields **6.95%**, up from a **5.65%** low at issuance last autumn. The piece is careful: this is a modest sell-off, not a crisis, and partly reflects rising Treasury yields lifting all yields.
