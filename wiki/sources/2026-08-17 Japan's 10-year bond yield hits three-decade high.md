# Japan's 10-year bond yield hits three-decade high

Japan's 10-year JGB yield touched **2.93%** on Monday — highest since **1996** — approaching the psychologically and fiscally loaded 3% line as a weak yen fuels inflation expectations and markets price a September Bank of Japan hike.

Key facts:
- **3% is "a critical defence line for fiscal credibility"** (Shoki Omori, Deutsche Bank): it's the assumed interest rate in the government budget. Crossing it "would signify an interest rate rise unforeseen by the government."
- The yen: fell to a 40-year low of **¥164/$**, recovered to almost ¥155 after **co-ordinated US-Japan interventions** at the end of last month, and has since given back half the gains to **~¥159**. Inflation expectations near 2% "appear to reflect recent weakness in the yen" (SMBC Nikko), vs the BoJ's underlying-inflation estimate of ~1.2%.
- Oil prices from the **war in Iran** add to the inflation pressure — the same conflict straining the US-Korea alliance ([[2026-08-17 Seoul rattled by Trump's threat to drills]]).
- Complication: Q2 real GDP grew just **1.1% annualised** (expected 2%); "private consumption and capex declined" (Goldman). Yet a majority of investors still expect a hike to **1.25% in September**, with bets on an accelerating pace (LSEG).
