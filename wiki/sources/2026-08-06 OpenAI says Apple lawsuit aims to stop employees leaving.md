# OpenAI says Apple's trade secrets lawsuit aims to stop employees leaving

Michael Acton. [[OpenAI]]'s **formal motion to dismiss** — its first court filing in the case, two days after its public blog rebuttal ([[2026-08-04 OpenAI hits back at Apple trade secrets fight]]). Filed Wednesday 2026-08-05 in federal court in California.

**Key facts**
- Core argument: the suit is **"baseless and pretextual"**, an attempt to stem an employee exodus and "make up for [Apple's] shortcomings in the market for talent and retaining its employees, and its failures to integrate AI into its products."
- Legal hook: "California law and policy not only permit but encourage such employee mobility, which has been credited with powering the tech revolution."
- OpenAI says [[Apple]] **failed to identify any specific confidential information** allegedly taken, "as opposed to the sort of background any employer might ask about in an interview."
- On supplier information: OpenAI says Apple wrongly claimed it was proprietary when it was legally or publicly obtainable.
- On the Chang Liu allegation (electrical engineer accused of pulling secret files): OpenAI says the retrieval was **"requested and authorised by Apple itself"** after Liu was "hastily walked out of the door" on resigning.
- Adds that Apple **encouraged staff to use personal iCloud accounts for work**, "intermingling company and personal data," then failed to manage access on departure.
- Scale: "hundreds of Apple designers and engineers" have left for OpenAI since io Products' founding. (Earlier reporting said 400+ — see [[OpenAI–Apple trade secrets fight]].)
- Named individuals: hardware chief **Tang Tan** and **Chang Liu**. Apple has sent legal letters to dozens of former employees now at OpenAI. **Jony Ive is not a defendant.**
- Procedural: Apple sought an injunction earlier in the week; **hearing scheduled for October** before Judge **Edward Davila** (who presided over the Elizabeth Holmes trial).
- Product context: OpenAI acquired Ive's io Products for $6.4bn last year; is building a palm-sized, screenless AI device taking audio/visual cues from the environment. Apple partnered with Google in January for "Siri AI" — seen as a snub to OpenAI.
