# China eases limits on Nvidia H200 chips as AI race escalates

The control regime has inverted: the story here is **Beijing's import controls, not Washington's export controls**. The US has licensed Chinese groups to buy up to 100,000 H200s apiece (a chip two+ generations behind Nvidia's best); it's **China's NDRC that gates what actually enters the mainland**, to protect Huawei-led domestic chipmakers.

Key facts:
- ByteDance and Tencent each received **~10,000 H200s** in recent weeks; more approvals coming. Beijing wants most licensed chips kept **outside the mainland** — parked in Hong Kong, which lacks the data-centre power capacity to actually run them. "Everyone needs the chips but struggles to find a way to use them in Hong Kong."
- [[Nvidia]] has **~half a million H200s in stock** mainly for China, sales held up by *Beijing's* restrictions. Lenovo and other partners told customers last week they can resume H200-server orders, each purchase needing NDRC approval.
- Why Beijing is loosening now: domestic fabs can't meet training demand, and Chinese labs are racing **Anthropic's Mythos 5**. Moonshot's K3 last month "almost rivalled the most advanced US models"; Alibaba, DeepSeek and Z.ai followed at similar level — the gap has narrowed. Division of labour: domestic chips for inference, Nvidia for training.

Feeds [[China's open-weight AI push]] (the hardware leg of the self-sufficiency strategy — and its current limit), [[Nvidia]], [[China's economic slowdown]] (policy-driven tech investment). The Lex "cheap Chinese models strand US capacity" scenario gains evidence: K3-class models near parity ([[2026-08-01 Situational Awareness got the future right]]).
