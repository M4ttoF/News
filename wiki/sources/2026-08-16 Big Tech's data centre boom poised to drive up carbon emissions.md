# Big Tech's data centre boom poised to drive up carbon emissions

FT analysis of the 60 largest planned US data centres (Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta): once fully operational they could emit **101.5mn tonnes of CO2 a year** — ~7% of 2025 US power-sector emissions, equal to 27 coal plants or 24mn petrol cars ("a reasonable ballpark," per Jonathan Koomey).

The mechanism:
- **Three-quarters of utilities serving the 60 projects are planning/building new gas capacity**; a third of coal operators are delaying retirements. 17% explicitly told regulators the new gas is for a specific hyperscale data centre. Data centres are ~**55% of utility load-growth forecasts** for the next five years (Grid Strategies).
- US gas capacity in development **nearly tripled in 2025**; if all built, the gas fleet grows ~50%; over a third of that is for on-site data-centre power (Global Energy Monitor). Behind-the-meter generation for data centres is overwhelmingly gas (Rystad). Named cases: Entergy Louisiana building ~10,000MW of gas incl. **10 plants for Meta's Richland Parish centre**; Georgia Power 5,234MW gas + coal life-extensions to 2035+; Meta's El Paso and Richland Parish sites temporarily on off-grid gas.
- Admitted in the companies' own sustainability reports: Amazon emissions **+16%** y/y, Microsoft **+25%**, Alphabet's "ambition-based" metric +18%. All four lean on renewable-energy matching/credits; Greenstone (U. Chicago): "capital markets are going to force the firms to peel back some of their carbon mitigation efforts."
- Policy backdrop: Trump administration dismantling climate rules; Rhodium warns clogged interconnection queues could force *existing* gas/coal to run harder before new plants even arrive.

The El Paso site here is Sopaipilla — the same one insured for ~3% of value ([[2026-08-17 Meta and BlackRock's $14bn data centre exposes lenders to insurance gap]]). Starts [[Data centres - power, carbon and geography]]; feeds [[AI infrastructure financing]], [[Meta]].
