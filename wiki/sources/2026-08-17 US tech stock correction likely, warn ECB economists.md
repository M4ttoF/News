# US tech stock correction likely, warn ECB economists

ECB economists (blog post, 2026-08-17) argue a US tech-stock correction is **likely even if AI succeeds and current valuations are rational** — and that it could become "a question of financial stability for the euro area."

The mechanism, which is the interesting part:
- Early in a breakthrough, uncertainty is company-specific; pioneer stocks carry an **"option value"** that legitimately inflates P/E ratios.
- If the technology succeeds and diffuses, the uncertainty **shifts from individual firms to the whole economy** — and economy-wide risk "cannot be diversified," so investors demand a higher risk premium, which pushes valuations *down*. Success itself triggers the de-rating.
- Overconfident investors are an amplifier, not a precondition: "when overconfidence fades, prices can fall even more sharply than in the rational scenario."

Europe's exposure: euro-area households hold **~€440bn of US tech equities** (largely via funds); insurers and pension funds are heavily exposed to the Magnificent Seven; US and euro-area equities are historically highly correlated. "A US AI fallout would not remain a US problem."

Historical comparisons: 19th-century railways, 1920s electricity and radio, dotcom — "genuinely transformative technology attracted investment… valuations rose strongly before falling sharply." But boom-bust patterns are "only identifiable with hindsight"; prices could rise further, and if AI is transformative enough, valuations could be higher still *after* a correction.
