# Boehly's investment group rolls out AI across portfolio after taking stake in start-up

Todd Boehly's **$75bn** asset manager **Eldridge** is deploying AI across holdings from **Chelsea Football Club** to film studio **A24**, as investment firms move to defend portfolios against AI disruption.

**Key facts**
- Boehly's thesis on what survives: businesses with **"unique data, physical assets and irreplicable IP"** are best placed to weather AI. The strategy is meant to let portfolio companies "play offence" while overhauling their own investment processes.
- Concrete deployments: **Chelsea** uses AI to analyse football injuries to reduce time players spend sidelined; **A24** has a **$75mn research partnership with [[Google DeepMind]]** exploring AI in film-making.
- Co-founder **Tony Minella** has driven the AI strategy for two years; the framing is giving colleagues tools and skills to rethink all their workflows, and he concedes it will take "a lot of legwork."
- Eldridge structure: founded 2015 by Minella and Boehly (who previously worked together at Guggenheim Partners); **100+ companies** across defence, media, finance and sports; Boehly is Chelsea's chair.
- The move: Eldridge acquired a **50% stake in European AI company Sudolabs**, giving it access to ~**70 specialists including ~40 engineers**. Intended to streamline due diligence and other parts of Eldridge's investment process.
- Eldridge has also backed AI-wave companies including cloud group **CoreWeave** — but Sudolabs is about its own internal capability, not exposure.

**The detail that matters beyond this story — who is partnering with whom**
- **Blackstone** and **Apollo Global Management** have struck deals with [[Anthropic]].
- **Bain, TPG and Brookfield** have formed a venture with [[OpenAI]].
- These are largely the same private-capital firms now financing the AI build-out itself (see [[AI infrastructure financing]]) — they are simultaneously lenders to AI, deployers of AI, and owners of businesses AI may disrupt.

**On deliberately avoiding lab dependence**
- Eldridge had worked with Anthropic on similar projects but chose to buy into Sudolabs partly **to avoid over-reliance on a single US AI provider**.
- Boehly: "Single-sourced is never a good place to be" — drawing analogies to supply chains, energy infrastructure and the Strait of Hormuz — adding that as credit investors they know what being captive means.
