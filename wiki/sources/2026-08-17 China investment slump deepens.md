# China investment slump deepens as economy shows signs of weakness

July data (National Bureau of Statistics, released 2026-08-17) came in weak across the board, pressuring Beijing to deliver the fiscal acceleration it promised in July:

- **Industrial output +4.5%** y/y (forecast 4.8%; June 5.3%).
- **Retail sales +0.6%** (forecast 1.5%; June 1.0%) — trade-in subsidies for consumer goods are now a *drag* as their effect fades (Goldman: they'll "continue to depress retail sales growth through the rest of this year"; 2026 retail growth seen at just 1.5%).
- **Fixed asset investment −6.7%** for Jan–Jul y/y, deepening from −5.7% through June.
- Context: Q2 GDP grew **4.3%** — below the 4.5–5% official target band and the lowest reading since GDP reporting began in the early 1990s outside the Covid years. Exports (+23.9% in July) remain the main engine, but factory activity unexpectedly contracted.

The structural read (Conference Board's Yuhan Zhang): growth is "becoming more visibly policy-driven" — investment in information transmission **+26%**, air transport +15.7%, computer/comms/electronics manufacturing +7.8% while everything else shrinks. Policy-supported sectors can't offset property, private investment, and unsupported consumption. NBS blames extreme weather and a "complex and severe" international situation, admitting "the imbalance between strong supply and weak demand is relatively prominent."

Politburo response (July): "accelerate the pace of fiscal expenditure"; Xi Jinping acknowledged the need to "tap the potential of domestic demand."
