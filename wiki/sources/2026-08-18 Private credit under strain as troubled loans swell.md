# Private credit under strain as troubled loans swell

The FT analysis behind the FirstFT teaser ([[2026-08-17 FirstFT Trump's eleventh-hour demand to reduce drills]]): stress in the **$2tn private credit industry** is at its worst since 2017 (the oil-crash hangover).

Key numbers (FT analysis of Solve data unless noted):
- **Non-accrual loans at the 20 largest listed BDCs: median 2.8% of cost in Q2, up from 2.0% at end-March** — borrowers who've stopped paying or look likely to default.
- Fitch: private credit defaults hit a **new record in July**. PitchBook LCD: the biggest listed BDCs **shrank again in Q2** — impairments plus repayments outpacing new commitments (KKR, Blue Owl, Apollo's MidCap Financial among them).
- FS KKR: **7.1%** of its book troubled. Listed BDCs run by KKR and BlackRock down **>15%** over a year; Apollo's down 14.5%. BlackRock's TCPC sold a $523mn loan block and hired bankers to explore wind-down; KKR's vehicle waived incentive fees.
- Named casualties: Medallia (Thoma Bravo walked from a **$5bn equity cheque**; Blackstone marks the loan below 50¢ from 60¢), Cornerstone OnDemand (Ares writedown), Affordable Care (default; lenders took over).
- Oppenheimer's Mitchel Penn: bottom-quartile BDCs returned **less than the 10-year Treasury** over five years; sector "priced for death"; "underwriting wasn't as good as it should have been."

The two-sided read, preserved as stated: Golub's "We're in a credit cycle… I don't think there's a lot of denial any more" and Oaktree's defensive posturing vs Blue Owl/Ares insisting metrics are healthy and issues isolated — with several executives blaming media coverage (including the FT's) for the outflows. Epicentre: **2020–21 vintage** zero-rate LBO loans now unserviceable at higher rates. One AI hook: software firms are a large share of BDC books, and their revenue durability is uncertain "as corporate spending shifts to AI."
