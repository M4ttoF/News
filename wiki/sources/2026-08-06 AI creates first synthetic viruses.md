# AI creates first synthetic viruses

Clive Cookson. Stanford researchers used a generative AI model to design and build **16 synthetic phages** — viruses unknown in nature — the first time generative design has produced entire functional genomes. Published in *Science*, 2026-08-06.

**Key facts**
- The model is **Evo 2**, a "genomic language model" that writes new genomes (DNA instructions for life). Project leader: **Brian Hie** (Stanford); team includes Samuel King and Aditi Merchant.
- The synthetic phages killed *E. coli* **more effectively than the natural phage ΦX174** that served as Evo 2's template.
- Hie: "a proof of concept showing for the first time that generative design can generate entire functional genomes." He says the approach could extend to bacteria and later more complex life.
- Safety measures taken: non-pathogenic *E. coli* strain; the model was **not trained on viruses capable of infecting animal or plant cells**; work done in a secure lab.
- **Evo 2 is open source and free to download.** Hie argues the health benefits outweigh bioterrorism risk; the lab has no commercialisation plans (philanthropy/government funded).
- Countervailing view in the same *Science* issue — Thomas Inglesby and Moritz Hanke (Johns Hopkins Center for Health Security): the work "raises urgent biosafety and biosecurity questions" and "existing governance frameworks are insufficient for overseeing generative genomics."
- Adrian Woolfson (CEO, DNA-synthesis firm Genyro) calls it "biology's Wright Brothers moment."
- Application context: phage therapy is an antibiotic alternative long used in eastern Europe, limited in the west by complex matching procedures and resistance risk. AI could revive it as antibiotic-resistant pathogens spread.
