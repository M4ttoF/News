# The next China shock will come from open-source AI

**Opinion** (Lizzi Lee, Asia Society Policy Institute's Center for China Analysis) — commentary, not reporting; treat the framework as her argument. This is the piece flagged as un-fetched in [[2026-08-17 FirstFT Trump's eleventh-hour demand to reduce drills]].

The argument: China is building a **self-sustaining, internally reinforcing AI ecosystem and increasingly offering it abroad**. Her periodisation: China shock 1.0 = post-WTO manufacturing; 2.0 = cleantech (solar, batteries, EVs); 3.0 = digital-tech diffusion; **4.0 = exporting China's approach to innovation as a national project** — "the next shock will not arrive in a container ship."

Key claims:
- At the World AI Conference in Shanghai, Xi Jinping positioned China as champion of **low-cost, open-source AI as a "global public good"** vs the US alternative.
- US export controls were an **"own goal"**: they became China's organising principle. Precedent: Huawei post-2019 Entity List → HarmonyOS, MindSpore.
- Precision: Chinese models are **open-weight, not open-source** (weights public, training data not; Xi's term *kaiyuan* overstates it).
- Economics of the strategy: enormous AI spending needs paying customers; domestic demand is weak ([[China's economic slowdown]]), so **going global with cheap, fast-spreading models is a revenue necessity**, especially in emerging markets.
- Governance angle: open weights can't be revoked or re-safeguarded once released, pushing China toward **"selective openness"** for frontier systems. Governance authority is distributed and messy (Shanghai does evaluation infrastructure, Beijing risk frameworks; CAICT, standards bodies) — "but the technical capacity beneath it is becoming more substantive."
- Adoption consequence: countries running Chinese AI absorb Chinese technical standards and governance principles — infrastructure as geopolitical foothold.

Starts [[China's open-weight AI push]]. Connects [[China's economic slowdown]] (the export-or-die logic) with the AI beat's US-side financing story — the mirror of [[AI infrastructure financing]]: America's constraint is capital absorption, China's is customers.
