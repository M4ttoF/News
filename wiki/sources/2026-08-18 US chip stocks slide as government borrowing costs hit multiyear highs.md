# US chip stocks slide as government borrowing costs hit multiyear highs

The rates-vs-AI collision, live: chip stocks led a Tuesday selloff (Philadelphia Semiconductor index **-5.6%**, Nasdaq 100 -1.7%, Nvidia -2.2%, Sandisk -9%) as long yields hit multi-decade highs.

The yield tape: 30-year Treasury touched **5.34%, highest since 2007** (US debt nearing $40tn; last week's 30-year auction paid the most since 2011); 30-year Bund 3.78% (highest since 2011); French 30-year 4.9% (2008); UK 5.86% (near post-1998 high); **Japan 30-year 4.16%, near record** ([[Japan's bond market and the BoJ]]). Driver: Brent back above **$90** on the Iran war — "long-end yields have largely been following oil prices" (Jefferies).

The load-bearing quote — Barclays' Pradhan names three factors in the long-end selloff: **"the budget deficit outlook, AI-related corporate issuance and the changing Treasury buyer base."** AI borrowing is now a named cause of sovereign yield moves; Barclays sees record $1.9tn IG issuance in 2026. Same analyst, same thesis as [[2026-08-18 AI like a debt machine]] — this is the news event, that's the mechanism piece.

Mechanically: higher long yields compress growth-stock valuations (the discount-rate channel the ECB described) — a small live rehearsal of [[2026-08-17 US tech stock correction likely, warn ECB economists]]. Amundi's Mortier: bond market "getting nervous" about fiscal trajectories; steepener trades crowding.
