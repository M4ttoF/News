# AI and white-collar work

Standing question: which knowledge professions AI actually displaces, which adapt, and how workers signal in the transition. Current evidence in this wiki:

## Displacement pressure

- **The layoff-attribution problem** (2026-08-19): 180k+ job losses "linked to AI" since May 2023, but the sceptical case is strong — overclaiming incentives, no macro signal (US claims at 1969 lows), AI-citing firms underperform the Nasdaq ~10% post-announcement. Real effects concentrate in entry-level hiring collapse, customer service, and **hiring suppression rather than firing** (postings -12.6% y/y UK) ([[2026-08-19 Is AI really responsible for recent job cuts]]).
- **Recruitment's signal collapse** (2026-08-20): 87% of UK recruiters see AI in half of applications; "when every application is polished, polish tells you nothing"; the market reprices toward costly signals — referrals and recommendation letters — at a diversity cost ([[2026-08-20 Why recruiting is going retro in the age of AI]]). Same cost-shifting shape as the letting-agent and bug-bounty cases below.

- **Strategy consulting**: analytic work (research, pattern-spotting, deck-making) is commoditized by LLMs; the surviving product is change-orchestration process, not intelligence. Industry expected to shrink; fees shifting from per-diem to outcomes ([[2026-08-06 Who needs consultants in the age of AI]]).
- Garicano's information-problem argument for why some advisory work resists AI: the data needed to plan transformations is tacit and only surfaces through human planning struggle ([[2026-08-06 Who needs consultants in the age of AI]]).
- [[Anthropic]] CEO Dario Amodei is the leading displacement-warner; his company's tools "triggered market ructions" over software-engineer and lawyer replacement fears ([[2026-08-03 Palantir forecasts greater US demand for AI software]]).

## Cost shifted, not removed

A recurring 2026 pattern: AI doesn't eliminate the work, it moves the labour to whoever receives the output.

- **Letting agents**: 90% of tenant complaints are now multi-page, statute-quoting emails that once would have been a phone call — so agents hire AI to summarise the AI-written complaints. Net effect is more work on both sides, not less ([[2026-08-11 UK letting agents under pressure from AI-assisted tenant complaints]]). See [[AI in courts and law enforcement]].
- **Security triage**: the same shape in vendor bug bounties — machine-speed submissions overwhelm human validation, so Apple rations submissions ([[2026-08-02 Apple struggles with AI bug hunters]]). See [[AI and cybersecurity]].

## Where using AI gets you fired

- UK policing has begun disciplining and criminally investigating officers over chatbot use on case material; one police staff member has been dismissed ([[2026-08-10 Senior UK detective under investigation for alleged misuse of AI]]). The professional risk runs in both directions — not using AI may cost competitiveness, but using it in evidentiary or confidential work now carries career consequences. See [[AI in courts and law enforcement]].

## Worker adaptation signals

- NBER "time travel" study: ≥1 in 5 LinkedIn users retroactively edits old job descriptions — playing up AI-tool use, playing down DEI — revealing beliefs about what the market now rewards ([[2026-08-05 LinkedIn time travellers and AI skills]]).
