# Progressive Democrat wins in Michigan in rebuke to party establishment

Progressive challenger Abdul El-Sayed has defeated congresswoman Haley Stevens in a high-stakes Democratic Senate primary in Michigan that has delivered a stunning rebuke of the party establishment.

The result is a significant victory for the party’s progressive leftwing and sets the stage for a blockbuster US Senate race in the critical swing state in November’s [midterm elections](https://www.ft.com/us-midterm-elections-2026).

The Associated Press called the race for El-Sayed on Wednesday morning local time after a vote count that went through the night. El-Sayed won by a razor-thin margin, with 48.5 per cent of the vote, compared to 47.5 per cent for Stevens, with 99 per cent of ballots counted.

“Great news for the Republican Party,” Donald Trump wrote on his Truth Social platform. “El-Sayed, a Communist loser who hates Jews and Israel is the projected winner in his race. Now the Dumocrats crazy policies will only get worse!”

As the Democratic candidate, El-Sayed will face Republican former congressman Mike Rogers in what is likely to be one of the most competitive and expensive Senate races in the country.

The bitter primary contest in Michigan had become a proxy battle for a wider fight over the future of the [Democratic Party](https://www.ft.com/stream/d3022c39-5296-4f1b-bb29-e104acd9e9ee).

El-Sayed, a 41-year-old former public health official, was endorsed by progressives including Vermont senator Bernie Sanders and New York congresswoman Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez.

Stevens, a 43-year-old four-term congresswoman, had the backing of a who’s who of the Democratic Party establishment, including the party’s Senate leader Chuck Schumer and Gretchen Whitmer, Michigan’s popular Democratic governor.

The Stevens campaign and groups supporting her outspent El-Sayed and his affiliated Super Pac by a nearly four-to-one margin.

But it was not enough to stave off the insurgent candidate, who centred his campaign on a push for universal public healthcare and fierce opposition to US support for Israel.

Stevens’s campaign touted her bipartisan record and argued that as a moderate she was in a better position to win over swing voters in the November vote.

Michigan is seen as a must-win for Democrats if they stand any chance of regaining control of the Senate. President Trump defeated Kamala Harris in Michigan two years ago by a razor-thin margin of less than 2 per cent.

El-Sayed’s victory is the latest in a number of primary wins for the progressive left, after a slate of candidates endorsed by progressive mayor Zohran Mamdani [swept Democratic congressional primaries](https://www.ft.com/content/c2ae8dcf-ac18-4683-a206-707024514649?syn-25a6b1a6=1) in New York City earlier in the summer.

November’s Senate election in Michigan will now be seen as a bigger test case for progressives, as they seek to convince fellow Democrats that left-leaning candidates can triumph in swing states that will be critical battlegrounds in the 2028 presidential election and beyond.

Polling averages compiled by RealClearPolitics in the run-up to Tuesday’s primary projected that Rogers would narrowly beat El-Sayed in a head-to-head match-up, while Stevens would defeat Rogers by nearly two points.

A third Democratic candidate, state legislator Mallory McMorrow, suspended her campaign last month but declined to endorse El-Sayed or Stevens. She endorsed El-Sayed early on Wednesday and called on the party to unite behind his candidacy.

“Take it from me, this was a brutal primary,” McMorrow said. “But whatever differences there were between the candidates in this race, those differences are nothing compared to the contrast in November.”

Tuesday’s vote laid bare [sharp divides among party members over Israel](https://www.ft.com/content/07aed955-d8c7-4ba4-b7b0-14e996bb9bc8?syn-25a6b1a6=1), which became a contentious issue in the final weeks of the increasingly acrimonious primary.

The United Democracy Project, a Super Pac backed by the [American Israel Public Affairs Committee](https://www.ft.com/content/889c551a-88ab-451a-87f5-cdec65467a64?syn-25a6b1a6=1), spent nearly $31mn supporting Stevens’s candidacy, according to federal campaign finance records — the group’s largest investment in a single race to date.

In the final stretch of the campaign, El-Sayed accused Stevens of being beholden to the pro-Israel lobby group. She responded by saying she was only “beholden to the people of Michigan”.
