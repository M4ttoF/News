# FirstFT: Trump’s eleventh-hour demand to reduce drills rattles Seoul

Good morning and welcome to FirstFT. In today’s newsletter:

* Seoul rattled by threat to drills
* China’s investment slump deepens
* A power crunch in India
* ‘The world’s most expensive passport photo’

---

We start with South Korea, where the nation’s security establishment is reeling due to an expected salvo from US President Donald Trump.

**What to know:** Trump made an eleventh-hour demand to the Pentagon to scale back forthcoming joint military exercises with South Korea because they [send a “hostile” message to North Korea](https://www.ft.com/content/8889375a-bcaa-4539-a000-c89b73dbcf4c?syn-25a6b1a6=1). He added that the drills were “inappropriate and hostile” to North Korea, a country he deemed “unthreatening and respectful”.

This year’s drills, scheduled to begin yesterday, were set to include 14 exercises over 11 days, ranging from drone attacks and GPS jamming to electronic and cyber warfare.

**Strained US-Korean alliance:** The US president’s intervention has deepened concerns about the reliability of its most important security partner, the latest in a series of tests to the alliance that has lasted since the Korean war.

While Seoul and Washington will publicly deny any impact on that readiness, “in private”, said John Lee of Korea Risk Group, “people will begin to ask uncomfortable questions”. Analysts also believe Trump’s move could be an overture to revive diplomacy with Pyongyang, a major unfulfilled priority of his first term.

### Five more top stories

**1.** **China’s industrial output softened last month and consumer spending was muted.** The weaker than expected data underlines the pressure on the country’s top leadership to fulfil promises [to accelerate fiscal stimulus spending](https://www.ft.com/content/09a8a200-e572-486e-9bff-1f57933aebfc?syn-25a6b1a6=1). China last month unveiled second-quarter growth of 4.3 per cent, one of its lowest readings in decades.

* **The next China shock:** The country is beginning to construct its own self-sustaining AI ecosystem — and is [increasingly offering it abroad](https://www.ft.com/content/2f705a5a-2c4e-4bca-b08a-ed9372ef3b2e), writes Lizzi Lee, a fellow at the Asia Society Policy Institute’s Center for China Analysis.

**2.** **Trump’s son-in-law Jared Kushner held talks with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu** yesterday, a day after meeting Hamas’s leader, as the Trump administration attempts to push forward its peace plan for Gaza. [Kushner’s meeting](https://www.ft.com/content/eea1da3e-1c1c-4957-ad9d-f0372b2694f0?syn-25a6b1a6=1) with Netanyahu comes just over a week after the Israeli leader publicly rejected a US-backed plan for the Gaza Strip.

* **Threats to Oman:** Trump has threatened to bomb Oman if the Gulf state [“gets in the way”](https://www.ft.com/content/d4d07198-498d-48d1-9e54-85f1febcd070?syn-25a6b1a6=1) of Washington’s efforts to strike a deal with Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.
* **Middle East realignment:** The war is [reshaping alliances](https://www.ft.com/content/895ca028-33c7-4295-bf8c-13a3121b80ac?syn-25a6b1a6=1) in a way neither America nor Israel foresaw, writes the FT’s editorial board.

**3.** **Exclusive: US financiers Todd Boehly and Mark Walter are in talks to sell their stakes in Chelsea Football Club** to majority owner Clearlake Capital, according to people briefed on the matter. [Any deal](https://www.ft.com/content/71a498fa-4ce7-4c0b-ab14-7b7f46b2b9c6?syn-25a6b1a6=1) would potentially help to resolve long-running tensions among the Premier League club’s ownership group, which acquired it for £2.5bn in 2022.

**4.** **Strain is spreading across private credit portfolios**, as the industry faces its [biggest challenge](https://www.ft.com/content/67acde0d-4154-4332-b33b-2d03d3a86007?syn-25a6b1a6=1) in almost a decade. The value of troubled loans held by some of the biggest private debt investors has reached levels last seen in 2017, when the industry was dealing with a hangover from an oil price crash.

**5. Nvidia has agreed to provide a more than $100bn backstop for a new OpenAI data centre** in Ohio in one of the biggest AI infrastructure deals yet. The AI chipmaker will also invest [$1.5bn into SB Energy](https://www.ft.com/content/82a4b183-7201-4789-95b7-e39e81c827bc?syn-25a6b1a6=1), a company founded as part of the SoftBank Group in 2019 that has focused on data centre development after striking a $1bn alliance with OpenAI in January.

* **New wave of wealthy:** The rapid growth of AI has created more millionaires and billionaires who are [now reshaping markets](https://www.ft.com/content/ac79a102-1620-48bf-a36b-416aac25de1d) for trophy assets such as property, private jets and high-performance yachts.
